Three stories from today that every business leader should be paying attention to.

Cursor AI is closing a $2B funding round at a $50B+ valuation, with NVIDIA joining alongside a16z. The company has over $2B in annualized revenue, 67% Fortune 500 adoption, and projects to hit $6B by end of 2026. Meanwhile, ChatGPT went down globally this morning for nearly two hours, affecting millions of users and enterprise workflows dependent on OpenAI's tools. And Maine just became the first US state to pass a bill freezing new large data centers for 18 months, with over a dozen states watching closely.

The through-line: AI infrastructure is simultaneously being valued at astronomical levels, exposed as a single point of failure for businesses, and now facing its first real legislative friction. All in one day.

My two cents: most companies are building AI strategies that assume the infrastructure will always be available, always be legal to use, and always be cheap to access. Today is a reminder that none of those assumptions are safe. Redundancy, regulatory awareness, and vendor diversification are not IT concerns. They are board-level strategy decisions.
